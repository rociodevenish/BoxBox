## Frozen experiment and public seam

The [standalone tool](../../../tools/ConstructionEvidence/README.md) materializes selected builds through public `ConstructedBaseCar.Apply`, `WeekendPreparation.Prepare`, `SimulationReplay.Capture` and `RaceEngine.ResolvePhase`. The primary observations are public race classifications, finishing times, tyre/eligibility records and incidents, not assertions about private coefficient arithmetic. Frozen inputs/results are in [the archive](04-experiments/construction.json.gz), [baseline comparisons](04-experiments/summary.json), [all pairwise comparisons](04-experiments/pairwise.json) and [content/runtime manifest](04-experiments/manifest.json).

There are **1,300 races**, each with 22 entries/11 teams: 300 normal races (2 circuits × 5 builds × 30 seeds) and 1,000 separately labelled opt-in diagnostic races (2 × 5 × 100). Each comparison changes only team-01's two cars. All 22 named drivers, their detailed ratings/preferences, opponents, grid and strategy stay fixed. This is one supplied roster, not 1,300 independent rosters or household studies. The target starts first/second; gaps are 0.8 seconds. Monza and Monaco use the supported representative two-segment geometry, not imagery or a validated full-calendar circuit model.

Every race has 20 laps, dry default conditions, neutral Weekend Setup, no permanent development/Race Package, Standard pace and a pit to Hard at Race Phase 2 for every entry. No qualifying is simulated. Normal seeds 101–130 retain default incidents off; lap/race-form variation are zero, stop variation .6 and passing variation 1 with traffic enabled. Paired finishing deltas have almost no seed variation because scoped noise largely cancels; 30 seeds are not 30 independent demonstrations of calibrated physics. Raw configuration/plan/grid are frozen per case. A paired seed does not imply an identical incident draw path after state divergence.

The baseline is the **purchasable cheapest legal 18-credit build**: front-loaded rotation / braking / robust power / low-drag thermal. It is distinct from the legacy neutral-82 preset and the unselected construction source baseline (82, Reliability 84). All builds use the shared frozen catalogue. The experimental post-personnel balance is explicitly 24 credits, leaving 6/4/3/3/4 credits for baseline/low-drag/cornering/tyre/reliability. This isolates opportunity cost; it is not an actual acquired household's balance or a new credit grant. Expensive builds require sufficient actual ledger balance in production.

Catalogue `construction-catalogue-v1/1.0.0/2026.10.09-preseason-car-build-v1`, mapping `bounded-detailed-deltas-v1`, car source `car-spec/1.0.0/2026.10.08-initial-model`, driver/track source `1.0.0/2026.10.08-initial-balance`, preparation `effective-weekend-v1` and `weekend-setup-v1` remain explicit. The provisional source 20-unit envelope is superseded by actual shared integer credits: complete builds cost 18–24. Diminishing returns apply the first two modifier points fully, subsequent points at half strength, capped at six. No stars become physics. Each materialized `evidence-base-v1/<build>` identity, detailed ratings, handling axis/version, source preset, conditions, strategy and seed are retained; these synthetic experiment identities are not purchase receipts.

## Findings, including negative results

Signed seconds below are the alternative's two-driver mean finishing time minus the legal baseline; negative is faster. All 30 normal paired races finish in each arm. These are descriptive outcomes for this fixed setup and strategy.

| Build | Credits | Credits left | Monza seconds | Monaco seconds |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Baseline | 18 | 6 | 0 | 0 |
| Low-drag | 20 | 4 | -3.023001 | -0.735394 |
| Cornering | 21 | 3 | -1.263397 | -0.565932 |
| Tyre | 21 | 3 | -0.330883 | +0.263970 |
| Reliability | 20 | 4 | +0.239192 | +0.074789 |

Low-drag has the largest circuit gain at Monza and remains fastest at Monaco. **The expected cornering crossover is absent.** Pairwise finishing-time comparisons show low-drag faster than every other tested build in all 30 normal paired seeds on both circuits, while costing one fewer credit than cornering/tyre. Cornering trails low-drag by 1.759604 seconds at Monza and .169462 at Monaco. This is unresolved local dominance, not a universally best build or a balance success. Race positions can tie or differ through traffic; all raw classifications and paired position differences remain available rather than equating every finishing-time gain with a place gained.

The tyre build shows a circuit weakness: its modest Monza improvement reverses at Monaco despite spending three more credits than baseline. At Monaco it also trails the cheaper reliability build by .189181 seconds. This single stop strategy does not isolate long-stint preservation or prove that a tyre philosophy cannot work with another strategy. Reliability spends two more credits than baseline and loses normal finishing time; no failure-risk benefit exists when the opt-in consumer is disabled.

The separate designer diagnostic uses the existing `local-incidents-v1` policy with failure hazard .00003 per running second, default component wear, authored circuit mechanical stress and no forced events. Profiles come from the same public detailed-car mapping: target Reliability is 85 baseline, 82 low-drag, 84 cornering/tyre and 86.5 reliability. No policy sees a future incident. Across **100 paired race seeds / 200 target-car starts per build per circuit**, every build has 16 target retirements on each circuit, with 84 paired races where both target cars finish in both arms. The observed retirement reduction is zero, including the 1.5-point reliability increase over this already robust baseline. Conditional finishing-time differences retain the same direction as normal results; they can be selection-biased and are not unconditional race-time estimates. This rare-outcome sample does **not** prove the existing consumer is inactive or reliability ineffective; it does not resolve small hazard differences or establish final calibration. All target/incident events and complete-result replay hashes remain available.

Only two representative circuits, one roster, one grid and one strategy are supported here. Full-calendar comparisons remain deferred until validated circuit content exists. No additional hypotheses were tuned to force a crossover after these negative results.

## Archive and verification contract

The compact archive retains all raw classifications, tyre states and eligibility decisions, target lap/pit/tyre events and every incident event. High-volume complete traffic/event streams are recreated on replay; their entire final public `PhaseResult` SHA256 is retained and compared alongside the stored outcomes. Replay loads only frozen inputs from disk, reconstructs preparation, checks versions/costs/materialized ratings/grid/plan/scenario coverage, and runs the public race engine. It does not reopen current content catalogues. The CLI refuses a nonempty output directory; contract checks preserve original bytes and reject altered schema, missing cases, cost, materialized car ratings, race-driver inputs, model version and outcome hash.
