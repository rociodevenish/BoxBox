## Contract

`PersonnelAcquisition.Open` saves the seed-derived initial order, all seven picks per team, the schedule version and cursor. Manager starts at offset zero; Driver, Race Engineer and Technical Engineer categories rotate their starting offset by one, two and three. Each category's second slot reverses its first-slot traversal. Seeded order uses stable identity hashing with ordinal ordering, independent of supplied team order or client arrival. The campaign's actual seed is passed through. Public campaign projections expose the saved schedule/current picker, without offers, future pool identities or private budgets.

A hire commits ownership, debit, receipt, advanced cursor and the next reserved offer in the same campaign conditional write. Only one active reservation exists; all unchosen people remain eligible in the frozen pool. Pools require mandatory category demand plus one spare, with a minimum of three, so even the final pick has two or three offers. The supplied 95-person pool already satisfies this requirement.

`PersonnelAcquisition.Scout` accepts a command identity, revision and authorised team. It replaces the complete current offer with disjoint alternatives; returned cards remain available for later picks. It spends exactly one token and records the new draw and receipt without advancing the pick cursor or debiting credits. When fewer alternatives than the current offer size exist, the projection discloses insufficient alternatives and the command rejects without changing offers, revision, draws or tokens. Refresh is read-only. Hire and Scout receipts have different fingerprints and share the same command-identity namespace.

`ScoutAllocation` freezes bounded tuning (`bounded-scouts-v1`): two base tokens, one extra for the later half of the initial order (for odd counts, the strictly later positions), maximum three. One-team campaigns have no late-priority bonus. Public domain callers can configure zero to three base/cap and zero/one bonus for paired evidence. Allocation is provisional; this ticket makes no empirical fairness or roster-strength claim. Ticket 05 owns that experiment.

Each original and Scout offer is a persisted numbered draw. Save validation checks schedule/cursor/assignments, draw identity and size, current offer matching the latest draw, command/revision counts, token conservation, ledger and uniqueness. Accepted Scout/hire retries return current saved state without another token/debit/draw. Recovery never reopens the catalogue or regenerates offers.

## Compatibility and scope

Rules now use `personnel-acquisition-v2`, schedule `carousel-rotation-v1`. Intermediate ticket-01 acquisition-v1 saves explicitly fail through the existing unsupported/damaged-save conflict, which says no saved data changed and directs preservation/restoring a compatible deployment. They are not silently reset, shuffled or upgraded. Preset campaign saves retain their established format. Ticket 03 owns affordable completion reserves and roster-ready lifecycle; ticket 04 owns AI/deadlines, actual personnel consumers and season handoff. These remain acquisition campaigns until those consumers arrive.

The API adds authenticated `POST /api/campaigns/{id}/acquisition/teams/{teamId}/scout`. The private projection includes token count, Scout availability explanation, cursor/total picks, draw count and policy versions. Current-device authority is checked before domain receipt handling. Both commands save before acknowledgement. Browser pending Scout commands survive reload and retry the same GUID/revision; Scout and hire controls prevent overlapping uncertain commands.
