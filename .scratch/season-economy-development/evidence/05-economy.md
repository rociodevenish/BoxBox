## Reproducible experiments

The [runner](../../../tools/SeasonEconomyEvidence/README.md) uses public acquisition, construction, income, option-specific personnel mapping, development, effective-car, race and championship operations. Seeds 101–124 were declared before the full run. The complete matrix is 24 seeds × 2 legal acquisition policies × 3 car contexts × 3 development arms = **432 counterfactual campaigns**, using only authored Monza → Monaco, six laps and one opportunity. Event-one results are reused across development siblings. All siblings preserve roster/build, strategy/grid, event seeds, opportunity/project identities and hidden development channel key. No favourable outcome was selected. The fixture's fixed grid and pit-on-phase1 strategy are controlled experimental inputs, not a championship skill-policy finding.

Only team 01 changes between cheapest-legal and highest-price-legal acquisition; all opponents buy their cheapest actual legal offer. Every hire preserves unique ownership and the completion reserve. Personnel comparisons in the minimum-car context use the same 18-credit construction. Speed and mechanical philosophy contexts attempt named 20/21-credit cars, so their prices are a disclosed confound. Unaffordable philosophy requests explicitly retain the minimum car and record the rejection. Save, reinforcement and complementary refinement are actual public decisions; rejected investments record their failure before an explicit Save. There is no top-up or silent conversion into saving. Reinforcement means speed on the minimum/speed car and handling on the mechanical car; complementary refinement is the opposite. This is a one-opportunity philosophy comparison, not repeated full-season stacking.

The [frozen archive](05-experiments/season-economy.json.gz) retains source pool/cards and acquisition draws/offers/receipts, exact construction confirmations, quotes/personnel/controls, ledgers, outcomes/modifiers, effective presets, complete race replay inputs, classifications, scoring awards and hashes. [Every seedwise result](05-experiments/summary.json), including failed/inconclusive rows, is retained. [Manifest](05-experiments/manifest.json) SHA256: `1262D9CB7E479709667EF387647B5CEA7E9F542EA79A5A449F3F7CE661EA68E4`. The archive exactly replayed all 432 cases through public operations without catalogue reads. Incident tuning was mapped from supplied content once; replay uses its frozen configuration and public starting-driver composition. Versions include frozen personnel prices/reserve, catalogue/source/construction mapping, `flat-event-income-v1` (+6 per completed event), `permanent-development-v1`, `normal-refinement-v1`, `normal-controls-v1`, `matched-specialists-v1`, effective/card, race/randomness and scoring contracts. No source stars or budgetUnits were reinterpreted.

Observed credit pressure is substantial under these provisional fixtures. Minimum-car economical teams retained 6–20 credits after preseason costs (mean 12.917); expensive-legal teams retained 0–2 (mean 0.375). All 144 expensive-legal investment attempts across the three contexts were rejected for insufficient credits, then explicitly saved. Their speed philosophy failed affordability in 22/24 seeds and mechanical philosophy in 24/24; these failures remain in the archive rather than being replaced with fictional credit. Economical minimum-car speed requests failed 5/24 and handling requests 1/24. Across 288 intended investment rows, 110 were accepted: 80 normal, 12 partial and 18 delayed. All 18 delays remain paid/pending beyond the final supported preparation, with zero final-weekend modifier and no invented completion. Final-event income still grants once to every team and opens no new action.

The hypothesis that buying a refinement necessarily improves the final weekend is **not supported**: some paid outcomes were delayed or slower, and normal compromises mattered. Reinforcement did not dominate complementary development at Monaco; neither is a general fastest strategy. Expensive legal personnel constrained later spending, but price was not assumed to be roster strength. Target hiring can change later market offers/opponent rosters, and philosophy prices differ, so cross-roster/base race differences are not isolated personnel causal effects.

Early-lead diagnostics retain the actual event-one leader, target margin to the strongest opponent before/after event two, and final-margin difference against the identical Save sibling. Original-leader margin differences across the matrix ranged −7 to +3 points (descriptive mean −0.0278 including Save rows). **The target-leading subgroup is empty.** These observations measure responses to a non-leading team's investment, not leader self-investment runaway amplification. That hypothesis is inconclusive, not passed. Two races and one action cannot establish season-long runaway safety, long-run saving value, stacked diminishing returns or release balance. Full-calendar experiments remain deferred to approved content/release work without inventing a dependency cycle.
