# Segment simulation contract

`Track.Segments` is an optional ordered repeating layout. Omission retains the aggregate compatibility model. A provided layout is nonempty and has unique stable segment IDs. The engine copies its collection before running.

Lengths and `RaceEvent.DistanceMetres` are metres; baseline times and effective penalties are seconds. Speed/Cornering demands are independent fractions in [0,1]. Tyre demand is a nonnegative multiplier on distance-based wear. Passing eligibility and difficulty in [0,1] are explicit layout inputs; encounter resolution is delivered by subsequent tickets. The layout's summed baseline time determines segmented pace; `BaseLapTime` continues to describe aggregate inputs.

`SegmentSimulation.Project` is the public, pure physics seam used by the race engine. Immutable `SegmentInputs` supplies attributes, seconds per attribute point, whole-lap tyre/pace effects and already-resolved variation. Whole-lap effects scale by baseline time share, with a positive minimum equal to one second per lap distributed by that share. Returned travel and exit durations are seconds; speed is metres/second; position is metres from the segment start. Projections stop at the segment exit. `PredictCatch` takes two projections and their forward physical gap in metres, returning seconds only when the catch precedes both exits. It neither samples randomness nor resolves passing.

Race resolution schedules every segment exit. Classification exposes normalized lap progress from cumulative segment distance, and gap seconds use trajectory history. Segment events include stable segment identity and cumulative metres. Five leader-lap phase boundaries drain simultaneous events ordered by driver ID. Pace changes preserve current position, affect the remaining segment and persist after stops. Warm-up is spread across the out-lap by time share. Degradation integrates travelled distance times each segment's tyre demand; segment boundaries alone add no wear. A lap's degradation penalty is retained across its segments and refreshed on a new lap or player phase change, preserving equivalent subdivision.

Ticket 01 establishes clear-air segmented races. Spatial traffic, passing visits and rejoin constraints are implemented in dependent tickets 02–04.

Ticket 02 adds cumulative metre trajectories and restricted queues. Catch predictions use the shared seam and expire at the earlier segment exit. Caught cars inherit the defender's constrained speed until a trajectory boundary changes it. Chains update from the front; pending catches at the current timestamp survive recalculation. Delayed grid departures are explicit boundaries. Numerically equal queued Classification finishes retain stable grid order, treating differences below 1e-8 seconds or 1e-10 laps as ties. Eligible passing and pit rejoining remain tickets 03 and 04.

Ticket 03 adds eligible passing. `PassingZoneId` defaults to the segment ID; equivalent subdivisions explicitly share it. Shared zones must have equal demands, passing settings and baseline metres/second, and be contiguous around the lap, including an optional start/finish wrap. One pair/zone/visit identity owns one cached result. A wrapped zone retains its identity across start/finish; an all-lap zone renews each lap. Recalculation, phase changes and equivalent subdivision cannot draw again within a visit.

Entry while within configured battle proximity or first physical catch creates an opportunity. PassingAttempt events expose identity and location, with Value zero; cached future outcomes and draws remain hidden. Overtake events occur at physical catch only. Attack/Defend modify eligible encounter chance and incur battle wear once for that opportunity. Difficulty zero leaves the track passing chance unchanged; difficulty one suppresses passing. Blue flags bypass ordinary passing eligibility and record separate events without a passing draw or battle wear. Contact order updates on resolved passes so tied spatial positions do not undo an overtake.

Ticket 04 records pit entry/service/exit at cumulative lap distance, always the start/finish coordinate modulo track length. A queued instruction takes effect at the next driver lap crossing, retains the configured lane/service loss, and preserves the current Pace Instruction after fitting fresh tyres. Rejoining yields to on-track cars at the same physical coordinate, including cars on different completed laps. Contact order follows the new defender after a stop, so queued tied finishes preserve the spatial order.

Segment exits, catches, lap crossings and pit exits at one timestamp drain before new entry encounters are evaluated or the next Race Phase accepts decisions. Events use stable driver-ID ordering; trajectory-dependent encounters are recalculated after the timestamp's trajectories have been applied. This prevents a pass against a defender's obsolete pre-boundary speed. Classification retains complete cumulative distance while contact tests compare coordinates modulo one lap. The chequered flag finishes each remaining car at its next crossing; simultaneous final crossings are drained together. Stop eligibility remains separate from the raw finish.
