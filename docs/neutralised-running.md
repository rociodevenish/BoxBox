# Neutralised running and restart (S09 ticket 04)

`RaceEngine.ResolveSafetyCar` validates and commits exactly the legal free-car pit choices, then resolves real travel using the existing earliest-event engine. `CommitSafetyCarChoices` remains a separate atomic, zero-time operation for its public contract and historical staging replays. New session operations call ResolveSafetyCar once; they never queue a second copy of committed or ongoing stops.

The authored default `SafetyCarTuning` is 180 seconds, with the active classification leader limited to a lap in 1.8 × Track.BaseLapTime and catch-up cars limited to 1.2 × that baseline. The following target is one second at the leader's controlled speed. Duration is finite, positive and at most 600 seconds; multipliers are bounded from 1 through 4, with catch-up at least as fast as the leader; spacing is positive and at most 10 seconds. These are game coefficients, not measurements of real races. The deadline is another candidate on the shared clock, drained with simultaneous events. A chequered flag overrides that deadline and every active car finishes at its next line crossing; all-retired completes immediately.

At every trajectory change, cars look for the physically nearest car ahead around the circuit, independently of lap Classification. Catch-up ends at the following spacing through an exact encounter timestamp; following cars match the slower speed. No distance or observed gap is assigned, and no racing pass, Attack/Defend attempt, contact opportunity or blue-flag unlapping is generated during neutralisation. Cars already closer than the target retain their order rather than being moved backwards. Lapped cars keep actual physical order and completed distance; the ordinary timing observation exposes lap deficits. Pit entry/rejoin and retirement can change Classification. Ordinary blue-flag and battle rules resume only in the next normal operation.

Tyre use consumes actual distance once, including partial segment boundaries, with Conserve demand during neutralisation. Component condition and stochastic mechanical-failure exposure continue over elapsed on-track running at Conserve demand. Stationary service/lane time retains the existing no-running-exposure policy. Error exposure pauses under the controlled pace; previously sampled exposure resumes in normal running. Forced diagnostics remain laboratory-only and may still force a continuing failure during a stop. Persistent damage survives stops and restart; no repair or restart tactic is added.

## Explicit pit route

`Track.PitRoute(MainRouteLapFraction, LaneTransitSeconds)` authors a lane from the next lap-end entry to a fractional lap rejoin. The fraction is strictly between zero and one and transit is finite and positive. Stationary service is unchanged and precedes lane travel. Position is stationary during service and then advances along the authored route to its rejoin coordinate; the same segment trajectories resume there, including a rejoin part-way through a segment. PitEntry.Value is route elapsed time including service for an authored route. PitService.Value remains stationary service. Effective Pit Loss is the actual comparison with a car that stays on the main route, not PitEntry.Value or a refund.

The opt-in developer laboratory authors a 0.2-lap bypass and 38 seconds lane transit on a 90-second circuit, with the unchanged 3-second service. On an otherwise fixed-speed lap this gives 41 − 18 = 23 seconds normal loss versus 41 − 32.4 = 8.6 seconds against the controlled leader, before catch-up/traffic. Public tests compare actual resulting Classification and fixed service; multi-seed strategic claims belong to ticket 06. No imagery determines geometry. A null route retains the legacy aggregate PitLaneLoss + service hold at the line exactly. **Every future live risk-enabled track must author its route before enabling Safety Cars**; existing aggregate PitLaneLoss is lost time and cannot be silently used as actual lane transit.

## Session and replay

Neutralised resolution produces the ordinary phase-result presentation gate, followed by a normal window anchored to maximum active fractional distance plus the configured normal span, capped at the finish. Public next-decision distance accounts for fractional distance remaining (rounded to three decimal places for display). Normal command membership excludes retired/finished cars. Household control rederives human responsibility at each boundary before filtering active teams, so teams whose two queued stops skipped the special barrier return for normal decisions. Normal AI and assistance omit retired/finished cars. Own-car legal/committed status remains available after restart.

New designer exports use `browser-disruption-replay-v2`; its typed SafetyCar operation resolves actual travel. `browser-disruption-replay-v1` remains independently verifiable with commit-only staging dispatch. Saved v1 staging session lifecycle migration remains ticket 05; incompatible outcome hashes are rejected instead of reinterpreted. An authored pit route adds `+pit-route-v1` to simulation model metadata. Null-route, incident-free S08 configuration, ordinary browser format, model/randomness versions and outcome serialization remain unchanged.

No supplied assets or card-to-physics mappings changed. Existing versions/provenance remain in [local incidents](local-incidents.md) and [the special window](safety-car-window.md). Live incident risks remain off pending ticket 05 and the evidence gates.
