# Segment experiments

Run `dotnet run --project tools/SimulationRunner -- --suite track-passing --runs 20 --output reports/segment-track-passing`. Optional positional arguments `40 7` select the initial paired seed; distance stays 40 laps. The suite rejects fewer than 20 seeds and conflicting fixture or strategy overrides before writing reports.

The bounded suite runs two 90-second, 5000-metre layouts, two 22-car fields and five controls per combination: segmented difficulty 0/0.4/0.8, zero-noise difficulty 0.4, and aggregate difficulty 0.4. Both layouts integrate Speed/Cornering demand to 0.5/0.5. Their local demands, lengths and passing-zone visits differ. Equal-all has identical driver and car attributes; mixed-car-fit alternates straight/corner strengths and includes small base Pace differences. The first strength pair has equal clear-air lap pace. These coefficients are experimental hypotheses. Two independent cases run concurrently with immutable input snapshots; seeds within each case and published cases retain their declared order. Concurrency and report writing are separate from the simulation-only benchmark in ticket 10.

Both plans start on Medium. Baseline schedules every driver to Hard after phase 4; the alternative moves driver-06 to phase 3. Seeds correspond across plans and controls. Lap variation is 0.2 seconds, stop variation 0.6 seconds, estimate error 0.04, race form zero and passing variation one. The zero-noise control sets all these magnitudes to zero, including passing variation: its fixed threshold of 0.5 makes this suite's chances fail, exposing queues and failed opportunities without ordinary random passes. Blue flags remain explicit.

`track-passing-evidence.md` reports actual Overtake events, attempted encounters, traffic loss and BlueFlag events separately. Pit-cycle position changes are separate strategy outcomes. `track-passing-summary.json` publishes settings, fields, plans, seeds, model/randomness versions, all variability statistics and eligibility/distance groups. Each case has full comparison replay inputs and one paired seed's final event/diagnostic history; regenerate other histories with `--replay`. Generated reports stay ignored; the concise published evidence lives under `.scratch/segment-race-model/evidence/`.

The runner retains the shared pure SegmentSimulation physics seam. While executing this suite, repeated projection validation at every traffic comparison proved expensive enough to motivate caching each car's resolved free trajectory. Segment entry, phase decisions, tyre changes and lap transitions refresh it through the same seam; current distance, constrained speed and remaining horizon are derived from the current trajectory. Existing transition tests and byte-identical retained sample histories verify behavior. Ticket 10 measures runtime formally.
