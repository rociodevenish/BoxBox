# Practice Assessment v1

`WeekendPreparation.Assess(preset, laps, setup, seed)` is the public deterministic simulation boundary. It validates and prepares the same immutable effective inputs as `Prepare`; it never advances a RaceEngine, consumes tyres, wears components or predicts qualifying/race outcomes. The result separates `Feedback` from designer-only `Diagnostics` (effective snapshot and exact handling compatibility). Session projections must select legitimate feedback explicitly and omit the replay seed and diagnostics.

The S07 review remediation removes exact `DriverFits` from normal private/Open GET and SignalR contracts entirely. The controller shows source preferences and Adaptability before practice, then the single recorded uncertain assessment. Saving another candidate changes effective performance inputs privately but supplies no new compatibility score or handling observation. Exact fits remain in the public designer simulation/replay contracts for engineering evidence.

The assessment model is `practice-assessment-v1`, layered on `effective-weekend-v1` and `weekend-setup-v1`. Preserved source input carries driver/track card `1.0.0/2026.10.08-initial-balance`, car `car-spec/1.0.0/2026.10.08-initial-model`, neutral base/package and `authored-engineer-v1`. These are provisional design sources, never card-star physics or real-world calibration. The supplied collection was inspected again: 28 driver originals and 23 track originals, with no new asset category. No new imagery is introduced: both-driver cards retain the stable IDs, portraits and readable fallbacks packaged by ticket 02. Existing originals remain unchanged.

The circuit observation compares the existing mean straight and corner demands, describes the Aero compromise and reports no hidden opponent or future outcome. Both drivers receive a separate handling observation from the shared Mechanical setting. Their existing preference mismatch and Adaptability mapping remains the sole performance consumer; this assessment adds no pace bonus.

Information quality is an authored tuning hypothesis: average engineer Setup and driver Setup Feedback (each 1–100); High confidence at 80+, Moderate at 55+, otherwise Low. An uncertainty radius of `3 + (100 - quality) * .2` compatibility points narrows as information improves. A stable SHA-256 observation keyed by assessment model, seed and driver ID perturbs exact compatibility within that radius, clamped to 0–100. Observed handling is Comfortable at 90+, Manageable compromise at 80+, otherwise Preference mismatch. The visible uncertainty text accompanies the category; confidence is information quality, not certainty of a future result. These numbers need player comprehension and balance evidence; they are not empirical claims. Better information changes the observation/confidence only, never effective race attributes or exact underlying fit.

One confirmed provisional setup is assessed. The session saves complete inputs, seed, output and model/content versions before acknowledging. An identical command retry recovers the saved receipt after refresh/restart; a different command cannot buy another assessment. Later candidate setup revisions recompute effective inputs from the preserved base while the original feedback remains attached to its confirmed setup. Final-Ready and Qualifying Lock are subsequent ticket responsibilities.

Focused public simulation tests cover deterministic replay, separation of feedback/diagnostics, two-driver compromise, unchanged source/race inputs and information quality without performance gain. API/recovery checks and phone interaction evidence are recorded in ticket 03. Local checks do not establish cloud deployment, human comprehension or real-world calibration.

## Final confirmation and future qualifying freeze

Ticket 04 adds `assessed -> final-ready` confirmation of the saved candidate, with an explicit `final-ready -> assessed` reopen operation that withdraws readiness. Both transitions increment the team revision and preserve the complete original assessment. Direct edits while final-ready reject; reopening permits revision without buying another assessment. Authenticated controller commands carry a GUID, team revision and assignment revision. Current identity/device/assignment authority is checked before lookup of canonical GUID receipts. An identical accepted retry returns its original acknowledgement even after reopening or lock; it never reapplies a transition. Changed payload or operation conflicts.

`SessionCoordinator.FreezePreparationForQualifying` is the application operation for the later S08 qualifying integration. The authenticated host API `/api/lobbies/{id}/preparation/freeze` exercises this operation and returns a receipt/context identifier. S07 adds no qualifying UI, attempts, qualifying resolution, race engine or deadline. Every one of the eleven catalogue teams must be final-ready; an incomplete human/AI field leaves the entire preparation unchanged. Ticket 05 supplies automatic preparation; ticket 04 does not invent an AI policy or silently freeze only human assignments.

The freeze stores one immutable context containing all eleven team identities, preparation revisions, human/automatic source, original preset/base/package/content versions, final setup and effective inputs/model versions. One durable conditional mutation stores the context, locked team states and original command receipt together. The lobby becomes `preparation-locked`. Future qualifying consumes `FrozenPreparation` rather than reopening live content or recalculating from changed assets. A new freeze command cannot replace an existing context. Existing accepted team commands remain receipt-only retries after lock; new setup, practice, confirmation and reopen commands reject.

Public preparation exposes readiness/lock flags only; private team snapshots retain the original assessment, two-driver summary and lifecycle. Intentional Open-family projections remain explicit. Confirm/reopen/freeze publish saved state through the existing revision-qualified SignalR groups only after persistence succeeds. Failed writes restore the complete durable state, including receipts, and publish no success. Commit-then-error recovery can discover the accepted result, and a retry returns its original receipt. Ordinary authenticated GET and SignalR subscriptions recover the same preparation after restart. Shared-tablet final confirmation clears private content immediately after acknowledgement, before handoff.
