# Local incidents laboratory, version 1

This is an opt-in simulation model. Live weekends retain null `RaceConfiguration.Incidents`; enabling live risks depends on S09 tickets 02–04. No Safety Car window, neutralised period or live controls are enabled here.

`IncidentTuning` rates are hazards per **running second**, contact probability per actual encounter, component condition a fraction, and damage seconds per lap. Error and mechanical channels integrate elapsed exposure, retaining it across segment exits, traffic recalculation and observations. Their exponential thresholds use SHA-256 identities containing `local-incidents-v1`, seed, driver, channel and ordinal. Contact uses the actual attacker/defender encounter identity. No segment identifier enters error or mechanical draws. An observation never advances the clock.

Tyre and component condition are sampled at lap entry for that lap's hazard rates; this is an explicit coarse approximation. Changes of Pace Instruction apply prospectively to exposure. Push/Standard/Conserve operating demand multipliers are authored hypotheses 1.8/1/0.55. Error hazard multiplies demand, `(2 - ErrorControl/100)` and `(2 - lap-entry tyre condition)`. Mechanical hazard multiplies demand, stress, `(2 - Reliability/100)` and `(2 - lap-entry component condition)`. Component wear integrates actual running seconds, demand and stress, including slow damaged running and excluding pit-lane/service time. Equivalent geometry subdivision cannot create hazard opportunities. Rates and wear are independently zeroable. Error rate is bounded at 0.01/second to exclude time-loss feedback dominating forward progress; failure rate and wear are bounded at 1/second, stress at 2, and contact probability at 1.

`WeekendPresetCatalog.IncidentInputs` snapshots detailed Error Control and Reliability (1–100 design points), initial condition 1, content schema/data versions and track stress into immutable race inputs. Track `raceCharacteristics.mechanicalStress` is an ordinal 1–5 supplied design input; the authored normalization maps 1/2/3/4/5 to 0.5/0.75/1/1.25/1.5 operating stress. These are tuning hypotheses, never conversions from headline card stars or real-world calibrated rates. Supplied content versions are `1.0.0/2026.10.08-initial-balance` and `car-spec/1.0.0/2026.10.08-initial-model`.

Lock-ups cost 2 seconds and 0.03 tyre condition. Spins cost 8 seconds, 0.08 tyre condition and one second/lap damage. Contact costs each involved car 5 seconds, 0.08 condition and one second/lap damage. Persistent damage caps at four seconds/lap. Mechanical failure retires the car. Tyres and component condition clamp at zero. Ordinary pit stops replace tyres only; damage and component wear remain. Events apply once at their authoritative timestamp. Retired progress freezes; classification orders distance/progress, retirement time and stable driver ordering. Retired cars require no instructions.

Public phase events report observed facts, damage and retirement. Team condition observations expose coarse Good/Worn/Poor and Low/Elevated/High bands. Exact condition, exposure and draws are available only at explicit designer diagnostic operations. `IncidentScenario` is a separate lab constructor input, never a player decision; forced contact still requires an actual encounter. It is diagnostic evidence, not a simulation-frequency claim.

Null optional extensions are omitted from JSON so historical aggregate, segmented and standing-start replay hashes remain stable. Incident configuration and profiles are captured with new opt-in replays; historical records are not rewritten. No image assets are required by this simulation-only slice.

The earliest-event hook adds the next incident timestamp to each car's ordinary event candidate before advancing shared time. All cars accrue exposure to that timestamp before local consequences mutate trajectories. Ticket 02 can stop this same event loop after draining simultaneous events; it must not simulate a whole phase and roll it back.
